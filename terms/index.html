2What Fajr Wake does

  • Fajr Wake calculates Fajr prayer time for the location you set, with the calculation method you choose, and sets an alarm that stops when you complete a challenge.
  • Prayer times are calculated astronomically and may differ by a few minutes from your local mosque's timetable. Choose your country's method and use Fajr correction to match it. You remain responsible for confirming prayer times.
  • Challenges confirm that you got up and moved. They cannot prove who completed them, and they do not verify wudu or prayer. Some can be fooled (for example, a photo of your sink shown on a screen).
  • Features marked as being tested (such as push-ups) are experimental and may not work for everyone.

3Alarm reliability

Whether an alarm rings depends partly on your phone: its permissions, volume, battery and power-saving settings, and the limits of iOS and Android (for example, iOS does not ring alarms of apps that are hidden or locked behind Face ID, and some Android phones hold alarms to save power). Follow the setup steps in the app and on the support page, and use the test alarm to check that it rings on your phone.

An emergency stop is always available, so the alarm can be ended even if a challenge cannot be completed.

4Health and safety

Some challenges ask you to walk or exercise just after waking. Turn on a light, watch where you walk, and do only what you are able to do safely. Do not do push-ups or other exercises if a health condition prevents it, and stop if you feel pain or dizziness. Fajr Wake does not give medical advice.
